# Course sources

The three SecAware courses rest on these pages, all opened on 2026-11-06. Before launch, L-10 opens each one again and checks every claim against it.

These sources are guidance for reference. The course text is our own wording, so check the source itself before relying on a claim.

## Where sources disagree

| Topic | What the sources say | What the course does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Password length | NCSC: three random words, with a 13-letter example. CISA: at least 16 characters, or 4-7 words. NIST: services must require at least 15 for single-factor passwords | Several random words, at least 15 characters |
| Writing passwords down | NCSC: acceptable with care. CISA: use a manager rather than write them down | Manager preferred; paper can suit some people if locked away |
| Spelling mistakes | NCSC 2022 and CISA now say they are unreliable. CISA 2021 still lists them | Teaches them as weak evidence only |
| Padlock and https | CISA 2021 says to look for them as a sign a site is secure. They show encryption, not honesty | Not mentioned at all |
| Public Wi-Fi | CISA 2021 says to avoid it. No NCSC page opened covers it | Softer: use mobile data for sensitive things if you can |
| Second factor first or unique passwords first | NCSC password manager page calls 2SV the first and most important step | Teaches unique passwords first for flow, and says to start with email |

## Advice not stated directly in a source

These follow from the sources, but no page opened states them directly, so review them first.

- **Do not read out a code, and refuse an approval you did not start.** This follows from NCSC saying secrets and approvals can be relayed during a live session, but no page opened gives this instruction.
- **Reading a web address by the part just before the ending.** The `account-help.example.net` example explains CISA's point about lookalike domains.
- **Do not change passwords on a schedule.** NIST says services must not require this. Applying it to individuals is an inference.
- **Change a password if an approval appears unexpectedly.** Inferred, as above.
- **Lock the phone first, then erase if you do not expect it back.** The sources describe both features but give no order.
- **Keep your own charger where possible.** This softens CISA's 'use only trusted chargers and cables'.
- **Fraud reporting service.** The sources name national services. The course says 'the police or your country's fraud reporting service' to stay neutral, so each country's learners would need to find theirs.

## Age and status of sources

- CISA's Secure Our World pages show an 'Archived Content' notice saying the archive may hold outdated information. NCSC and NIST were preferred wherever they cover the same point.
- Several NCSC consumer pages were last reviewed between 2021 and 2022. The passkey pages, and the password manager page updated on 21 May 2026, take priority where they differ.
- These sources are from the UK and US. No source from another region was checked.
